Every living cell carries DNA, a long code written with four letters: A, T, G and C. The order of those letters is the recipe for building life.

To use a recipe, the cell first copies DNA into RNA. This step is called transcription. The copy is almost identical, except every T becomes U.

Finally, the cell reads RNA three letters at a time. Each group of three, a codon, adds one amino acid to a growing protein. This step is called translation.

Your results

01

Sequence length :

The number of bases (letters) in your DNA. Every base is one A, T, G or C.

02

GC · AT

GC percentage tells you how much of your sequence consists of G and C. G and C pair with three hydrogen bonds (A and T use two), so GC-rich DNA is more stable. AT is always 100 minus GC.

03

Reverse complement

The opposite strand, read backwards. A pairs with T and G pairs with C.
